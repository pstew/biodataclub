A hackathon is an event in which participants come together to develop software, analyze data, build tools, and solve problems during a short, intensive period of collaboration. Projects may involve data science, software development, artificial intelligence, machine learning, research informatics, data visualization, or other innovative approaches. 
[Here is a short article](https://www.rasmussen.edu/degrees/technology/blog/what-is-a-hackathon/) describing what you can expect. 


## Participation & Team Formation

Participation is completely voluntary; however, we encourage anyone with an interest in data, software, analytics, or innovation to consider joining. The event is intended to be collaborative, educational, and enjoyable, with a small prize awarded to the winning team. Since work on hackathon projects will occur during an intensive 1.5-day period, please obtain permission from your supervisor or PI before registering. 

- Hackathon teams are organized around projects.
- Each project will be led by a team leader (typically the project submitter).
- Participants will select their preferred teams during registration.
- Team leaders are responsible for coordinating project activities and delegating tasks.
- Participants will be matched with their project team approximately three days before the start of the hackathon.
  - Project leaders are encouraged to onboard team members before the event and ensure any required setup tasks are completed in advance.
- Participants of all experience levels are welcome.
  - Programming experience is helpful but not required. Many projects benefit from contributions in data curation, validation, visualization, documentation, project coordination, and presentations.
- Teams are encouraged to use reproducible and collaborative workflows, including version control, shared documentation, and well-documented analysis pipelines.

## Rules
- Teams will have approximately 1.5 days to develop a deliverable product.
- Each team will have 5 minutes to present its final product on Friday. Product demonstrations and presentation slides are both acceptable.
- A panel of judges will select the winning project.
- The top projects will be evaluated based on:
  - Reproducibility
  - Usability
  - Scientific and/or operational value
- No work (e.g., software development or data analysis) should be performed prior to the official start of the hackathon.
  - Pre-training models, pre-processing data, preparing datasets, or setting up existing infrastructure is permitted.
  - Addition of completely new features to existing code will be acceptable, but prior work must be disclosed. 
- Projects may utilize artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning tools.
  - Teams must document the AI tools, models, prompts, and datasets used in their work.
  - AI-generated outputs should be reviewed and validated by team members.
  - All use of AI must comply with institutional policies and data privacy requirements.

- Participants agree to follow the [code of conduct](https://github.com/pstew/biodataclub/blob/master/meetings/2026-12-10_Hackathon/CoC/code_of_conduct.md).
